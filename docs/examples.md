Under the landmark ***House v. NCAA* settlement**, the rules governing college athlete compensation and retroactive pay are as follows:

### 1. Direct Payments to Athletes (2025–26 Revenue Sharing Cap)
* **The Cap:** For the 2025–26 academic year, participating Division I schools are permitted to pay student-athletes directly up to an annual revenue-sharing cap of **$20.5 million per school**. 
* **How it is Calculated:** This cap represents **22%** of the average annual athletic department revenues of the "Power Five" conferences (ACC, Big Ten, Big 12, SEC, and Pac-12).
* **Future Growth:** This cap is not static; it will increase by roughly 4% (about $1 million) each year over the 10-year settlement term, reaching an estimated $32.9 million per school by the 2034–35 academic year. 
* **Participation:** Sharing this revenue is voluntary, and individual schools can decide how to allocate this pool of money among their sports, though competitive pressures have driven widespread adoption. *(Note: In the first year of implementation, 297 Division I schools shared a combined total of $1.77 billion directly with their athletes.)*

### 2. Back-Pay Damages to Former Athletes
* **Total Damages Amount:** The NCAA and its conferences agreed to pay a total of **$2.78 billion** (often rounded to **$2.8 billion**) in retroactive damages.
* **Who Receives It:** This fund is designated for over 100,000 former Division I student-athletes who competed between **2016 and 2024/2025** (prior to the settlement's final approval in June 2025) and were previously barred from earning money from their Name, Image, and Likeness (NIL) or other direct compensation.
* **Payout Timeline:** The damages are being paid out over a **10-year period** (amounting to roughly $280 million annually). This is funded through a combination of NCAA reserve funds, insurance, and reductions in future revenue distributions to all 32 Division I conferences.
* **Distribution Allocation:** The distribution of the back-pay damages heavily favors high-revenue-generating sports. Approximately **90% to 95%** of the $2.8 billion fund is allocated to former football and men’s and women’s basketball players, with the remaining 5% to 10% distributed among athletes of all other Division I sports.
